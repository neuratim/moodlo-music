# 100 Mood-Lifting Music Prompts

## Usage

Copy one complete bracketed tag line and the seven paragraphs beneath it into your music generator. Titles and the character-count lines are reference information, not part of the copy-ready input. Each full input is under 3,000 characters, counting its tags, spaces and line breaks. Every prompt is standalone; there is no shared instruction block you must append.

## Age and content

Suggested ages describe a creative programming focus, not a listening restriction, developmental assessment or promise of personal preference. All 100 briefs request clean, family-friendly material. The ten groups each contain ten prompts: 0-3, 4-7, 8-12, 13-17, 18-29, 30-44, 45-59, 60-74, 75+, and All ages. Adult vocals are specified throughout the vocal selections; these prompts do not request recordings of children. For children, an adult should preview generated audio and choose an appropriate playback level.

## Variety

The seven content areas are concept, rhythm, instruments, harmony, arrangement, voice and production. Each prompt has a different ordering of these areas and independently written sentences. Musical variety also comes from different lead instruments, motifs, grooves, chord movement, forms, vocal approaches and endings. Wording and ordering alone do not guarantee distinct generated audio; actual results depend on the generator. No specific artist, recording or existing melody is used as an imitation target.

## Filters

Energy is a relative creative label: 1 = very gentle, 2 = easygoing, 3 = buoyant, 4 = energetic. It is not a volume setting or a scientific measure. Vocal modes are Instrumental, Wordless and Lyrical. English lyrics are requested for lyrical entries; wordless entries use non-lexical sounds. Tempo, meter, key and target duration are composition requests, not guaranteed output properties. BPM is counted in quarter notes unless the prompt explicitly specifies dotted-quarter beats; the 7/8 entry explicitly uses quarter-note BPM.

## Review

These are creative briefs intended to sound pleasant or uplifting, not treatment instructions or guarantees of a better mood. Preview results for unexpected lyrics, sudden sounds, repetition and musical quality before sharing. For variety, keep successful versions that differ musically, not merely in their titles or prompt wording. A generator may ignore tags or theory details; the prose repeats the important musical directions.
